After-hours HVAC calls.

How to answer, triage and book no-cooling and no-heat calls at night — without burning out your team.

By Taha Mujtaba, Founder of Zynvox

The short answer

After-hours HVAC calls need three things: an instant answer so the customer doesn’t call the next company, clear emergency rules so real emergencies reach your on-call technician in minutes, and next-day booking for everything that can wait.

Your options

  • Voicemail. Cheapest, but most people with a broken AC or furnace don’t leave a message — they call someone else.
  • On-call tech answers the phone. Fast, but your technician is woken by every call, including ones that could wait until morning.
  • Answering service. A person answers and takes a message or pages your tech, usually billed per call or per minute.
  • AI receptionist. Answers or texts back in seconds, asks your triage questions, pages your on-call tech only for real emergencies and books the rest.

The triage questions to ask

  • What is the system doing: no cooling, no heat, strange noise, leaking water?
  • Is anyone at home who is elderly, very young or medically vulnerable?
  • What is the temperature inside, roughly?
  • Is there a gas smell or a carbon monoxide alarm? (If yes: leave the house and call 911 first.)
  • What is the service address, and is it in your service area?

Emergency rules worth writing down

  • No heat below a temperature you choose.
  • No cooling above a temperature you choose, especially with vulnerable people at home.
  • Water leaking from the indoor unit.
  • Gas smell or CO alarm: safety instruction first, then your team.
  • Everything else: booked into the first open slot, with a confirmation text.

Escalation that actually works

  • Page the on-call technician by phone call and text at the same time.
  • If nobody accepts within a few minutes, page the backup, then the owner.
  • Tell the customer someone is coming only after a person has accepted, with an honest arrival time.
  • Never put a real emergency into tomorrow morning’s calendar.

The next morning

  • Review every overnight conversation in one place.
  • Confirm the routine jobs that were booked overnight.
  • Hold review requests until daytime — no texts at night.
  • Add any customer who agreed to it to your spring or fall tune-up reminders.

Should every after-hours HVAC call be treated as an emergency?

No. Set clear rules — for example, no heat below a temperature you choose, or no cooling with children or elderly people at home. Those go to your on-call technician; everything else is booked for the next open slot.

What should a caller do if they smell gas?

Leave the house and call 911 or their gas company from outside, before anything else. Your after-hours process should say this first, then alert your team.

What if the on-call technician doesn’t answer?

Have a backup. Page the on-call technician by call and text; if nobody accepts within a few minutes, page the backup, then the owner. Only tell the customer someone is coming once a person has accepted.
